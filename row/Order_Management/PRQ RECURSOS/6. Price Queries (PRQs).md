## Introduction

### What is a Price query and what are we going to learn today?

This training outlines the process for handling price queries (PRQs) within the CC&T department.

### What is the purpose of the process and why is it needed?

The purpose of this internal business process if for CS and CC&T teams collaborate to investigate and resolve pricing- related discrepancies or concerns. This process typically arises in situations where there is uncertainty, inconsistency, or a need for clarification regarding the pricing of a product. A price query often acts as a temporary hold or blocker, preventing a customer purchase order (PO) from being finalized or booked until the issue is thoroughly reviewed and resolved.

### Outline the topic and expected outcomes from the training

Common reasons for a price query may include incorrect pricing on the PO, expired contracts, special pricing agreements, or issues related to account structure. The primary objective of the price query process is to ensure accuracy, compliance, and customer satisfaction before proceeding with order fulfillment.

## Process Overview

### Overview

A price query arises when Customer Service identifies a discrepancy between the prices on a customer's Purchase Order (PO) and those reflected in ORACLE. The most common reasons for such discrepancies are: CWPP (Customer Wrong PO) – Customer has incorrect pricing on their PO.

SNC (Sales No Contract) – No sales contract exists for the item.

SMI (Sales Missing Item) - Priced item / FOC item is missing from the contract.

SEC (Sales Expired Contract) – The sales contract has expired.

Please note that these are not the only reason codes, but they are among the most common. Refer to your Excel sheet ("Pending Reason Codes Improved Process and List") to ensure you are diagnosing the correct reason. Additionally, consult the file (“CS & CC&T Waterfall”) to determine the appropriate action to take for each code or situation.

### Requirements to Address a Price Query

Customer PO – Validate the details provided by the customer.

Oracle Sales Order (SO) – Confirm pricing and contractual information.

Contract/Quotation – To confirm correct pricing.

## Step by Step

### Customer Wrong Purchase Order (CWPP & CWNC) Occurs when there is a mismatch between the price on the customer's PO and ORACLE.

1. Open ORACLE, and search Sales Order to check pricing.

<!-- image: p6-img0 (page 6, 393x98pt) --> <!-- image: p6-img1 (page 6, 231x77pt) -->

2. Locate the relevant BSA (Blanket Sales Agreement) as circled above, from the sales order line.

3. Verify the price. From BSA vs Contract.

4. Communicate the correct price to Customer Service (CS) and provide the signed quote or contract as an attachment.

5. Reach out to the customer and request that they update their pricing in their system.

## Step by Step

### SMI (Sales Missing Item) Occurs when there is priced item / FOC item ordered by the customer but missing in the contract.

1. Open ORACLE, and search Sales Order to check pricing.

<!-- image: p7-img0 (page 7, 612x114pt) -->

2. Locate the relevant BSA (Blanket Sales Agreement) as circled above, from the sales order line.

3. Verify the item is not listed in the agreement and forgotten to be added in the BSA 4. If the item code is not listed in the contract, contact the KAM with a copy of the PO and request an addendum GMC.

## Step by Step

### Sales No Contract (SNC) Occurs when pricing does not populate for an item during order placement.

1. Open ORACLE.

2. Search for the related BSA or stand-alone modifier, by looking up the customers name and/or account number <!-- image: p8-img0 (page 8, 459x111pt) -->

1. If no results, check Box/H1 folder for the related quote.

2. If contract is located within H1/Box, complete pricing request form and send to the pricing team to load.

3. Once completed by the pricing team, advise CS to re enter the line and the correct BSA price will pull to book the order.

3. If no contract can be located, contact the KAM with a copy of the PO and request a GMC.

• SNC: No BSA results appear in ORACLE.

• SEC: BSA results appear but are listed as terminated or expired.

## Step by Step

### Searching for Pricing in ORACLE Locate Pricing Using BSA

1. Open ORACLE.

2. Search for the customer name/number and item code in the Sales Agreement tab.

3. Click Find to retrieve relevant BSA results.

4. Open the agreement and check: • Modifier Setup: Confirm validity dates and pricing.

• Discounts/Charges Tab: Verify the price.

### Locate Pricing Using Stand-Alone Modifiers

1. Open ORACLE.

2. Navigate to Advanced Pricing > Create Modifier.

3. Search for customer-specific modifiers using partial names or numbers with % symbols.

4. Open each modifier and use F11 to locate the item code and pricing.
